# Why "Trobar"

In the 12th century, in the langue d'Oc of southern France, a *trobador*
practised *trobar* — an Occitan verb meaning "to find", and by extension
"to compose". To *trobar* was to find a song: to hunt down the right words
and melody and make them your own. The troubadours wandered from court to
court with their entire repertoire on their backs, performing whether or not
anyone had asked them to — history is politely silent on how often they were
asked to stop. What mattered was this: the songs were *theirs*, and they went
where the singer went. Later, scribes gathered those songs into
*chansonniers* — hand-bound songbooks that fit a life's worth of music into
something you could hold.

That is what this app is. Not a stream you rent that vanishes when a
licensing deal expires, but a *chansonnier* you keep. You **find** the music
that matters to you, curate it into your own collection, and carry it —
losslessly, on your own terms — to your phone, your player, your SD card,
even where the signal doesn't reach. The troubadour owned his songs and took
them on the road. So do you.

And the logo is his lute, seen from up close: the carved rosette over the
sound-hole, an eight-point star ringed by twelve pommels, a nod to the
twelve-pommel Occitan cross. When your library syncs, the star turns, the
way a sound-hole seems to while the strings are ringing. Unlike his medieval
colleagues' audiences, you only hear what you asked for.

*Trobar: to find your music, and keep it close.*
